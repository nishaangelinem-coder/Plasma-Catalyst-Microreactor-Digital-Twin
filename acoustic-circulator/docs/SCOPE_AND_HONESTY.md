# Scope, assumptions and what was NOT done

* **No Cadence / VMware access.** The cloud session cannot reach the user's VMware
  workstation or any Cadence licence. Nothing in this repository was simulated in
  Spectre/Virtuoso. Instead:
  * circuit-level S-parameters of the time-varying network come from an in-house
    conversion-matrix (linear time-periodic) solver `sim/ltp.py`, which performs the
    same analysis as Spectre PSS+PSP and was validated against analytic sideband
    theory (0.01 dB), against ngspice (0.02 dB) and against coupled-mode theory;
  * transistor-level results come from **ngspice 42** with a 65 nm-class BSIM4 card
    written in the style of the ASU predictive technology model (`models/`). It is
    **not a foundry model**; absolute numbers (switch Ron*W, Coff, MIM density,
    gate capacitance) will change in a real PDK;
  * `cadence/` contains Spectre netlists, an OCEAN PSS/PSP script and a SKILL
    schematic generator so the user can regenerate every figure of merit in
    Virtuoso. `cadence/README.md` gives the step-by-step VM flow.
* **No fabrication, no measurement.** The paper's title, abstract and every table say
  "simulated". The resonator is an mBVD model with literature-representative
  parameters (X-cut LiNbO3 SH0, kt^2 ~ 24 %, Qm = 500 at 0.95 GHz); mismatch,
  spurious modes and temperature are not modelled.
* **Layout.** `layout/circulator_driver_top.gds` is generated by `layout/gen_layout.py`
  with a *generic* 1P9M layer map. It is a floor-plan-level tape-out candidate: no
  foundry DRC/LVS was run (no deck available). Device cells must be replaced by PDK
  pcells before sign-off.
* **Design pivot made during the study** (documented in the paper, Section III-B):
  the Estep-type parallel-mode loop with capacitive ring coupling was found, by exact
  analysis, not to work with acoustic resonators (ring capacitors collapse tunability).
  The final design is a series-mode resonant junction (wye) with switched-MIM
  modulators. The user's request used the word "varactor"; the switched MIM capacitor
  is a two-state (digital) varactor and the A-MOS analogue varactor is kept as the
  compared alternative (Table of topologies in the paper).
* **Comparison table.** Values for prior art in Table "Comparison with the state of
  the art" are from memory of the cited papers and are flagged in the table footnote;
  they must be checked against the originals before submission.
* **References.** See `docs/REFERENCE_VERIFICATION.md` for which DOIs could not be
  confirmed from the sandbox.
* **Linearity / noise** were not simulated (no two-tone PSS in ngspice harness);
  a voltage-stress-based power-handling estimate is given instead.

## Modelling lessons recorded during the study (kept for reproducibility)

1. **Parallel-mode capacitively coupled loop (Estep-type) does not work with acoustic
   resonators**: the ring capacitors multiply the susceptance slope of the resonator and
   destroy the tunability. The design moved to the series-mode resonant junction (wye).
2. **A switched MIM capacitor is not a varactor.** Modelling it as a capacitance that
   toggles between two values (lossless) over-estimated the performance by ~3 dB; the
   correct model is a hard-switched conductance (R_on / off) with the fixed MIM and the
   switch's off-state capacitance. The LTP solver has a `TVG` element for this and needs
   K >= 24 sidebands with the real gate edges.
3. **Switch parasitics to ground kill the junction**: with grounded gate and body the
   nMOS switch puts ~0.9 fF/um to ground at the star node and no circulating design
   exists. The RF-switch configuration (gate through R_g = 3 kohm, triple-well body
   through R_b = 20 kohm) reduces this to ~0.04 fF/um and restores the design.
4. **BSIM4 instance width**: in BSIM4 `w` is the total width and `nf` divides it; an
   early netlist used per-finger width and simulated a 300x too resistive switch.
5. **ngspice integration method**: `method=gear` numerically damps Q = 500 resonators
   and adds ~2 dB apparent loss; `method=trap` with 10 ps steps is required.
